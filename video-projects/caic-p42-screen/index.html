Step two
The screen
Some risks on your map are ordinary design problems. You'll fix those yourself in steps three and four.
Others touch health records, hiring decisions, or the law. Deciding those alone is doing a specialist's job without the specialty
Why the word “screen”
Same reason medicine uses it
A screening test doesn't treat you. It decides whether you need a specialist.
“I can handle this part, but I'm going to need to refer you to someone else for this part.”
Question one
What data is in play?
What the step can actually reach, not what it's supposed to use
Patients text their symptoms, medications, and billing complaints to a scheduling bot. This step can reach health information whether we invited it or not.
Non-sensitive PII
Name, email, phone, zip code
Handle it with care, but you can work with it
Sensitive PII
SSNs, bank accounts, licenses, health records
Different rules. Raw sensitive PII should not flow into an AI model.
Brightwater's message text just landed in the sensitive category
Question three
Who is the client, and what have they promised?
Every business carries obligations before you arrive: their industry sets some, their location others, their own privacy policies and contracts the rest
You inherit all of it the moment you touch their data

The division of labor
They tell you where the wall has to go. You build the wall.
The expert defines what must be true about the data. You filter fields, strip identifiers, encrypt a step. On the voice agent: the data must never reach the model provider, so we ran a local model.

Two signs the questions can miss
Katharina Koerner · purpose creep
Data collected for one purpose, used for another
Patient records gathered for treatment, now a list of diabetic patients for a marketing campaign. What a company told people at collection time draws a line around what it can do later. Repurposing is the moment to check with legal.
Aaron Alva · context crossing
Data showing up where its owners would never expect
The test: would the people the data describes be surprised?
Client hears the risk and says proceed anyway? Get that acceptance in writing.

The finding
The patient-message steps involve health data
A real engagement brings in a HIPAA-literate expert to define the data handling. Everything else on the map stays ours.
Keep the scale honest: a message-sorting system for a dental office with a human catching everything sensitive
