Why milestones, instead of collecting at the end
Two Reasons
01
Cash flowYou shouldn't be financing your client's project. A dragging project can mean months of work with nothing in the bank.
02
Forced agreement, mid-workSmall disagreements surface at the halfway payment while they're still small, not compounding until the end.
The done test pays its dividend
A milestone with a binary pass or fail done test is a milestone you can invoice
"Is it done?" should be a checkbox the client can verify alone. The opening story was a missing done test.
Employed track: review gates. Halfway demo, delivery sign-off, continued budget.
How scope actually erodes · one small favor at a time
"Sure, that's a quick change. I can knock that out real quick."
Individually harmless. Six of them: a lot of unpaid work and a blown timeline
Verbal favors leave no paper trail, no written trigger, no line
Drift is invisible without the written rule
In practice, it's friendlier than "change order" sounds
The V2 List
A running doc · shared with the client
Version-two ideas, where they can see it and edit it
Every mid-project "can you also do this?" goes straight onto the list.
At kickoff: "New ideas will come the moment you see the first demo. Great. Here's where they live."
Well-handled scope creep becomes a buying signal. Route it, don't absorb it.
