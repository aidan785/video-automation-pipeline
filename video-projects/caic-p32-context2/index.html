LangChain's four context failure points
How Context Breaks
01 · Poisoning
The info is wrong
A wrong fact gets in, and the model treats it as true. Defense: verification, a second and third pass on every fact.
02 · Bloat
Too much piled up
The window overflows and the model loses the thread. The one I've personally hit most.
03 · Confusion
Missing or off-topic
Tuesday's product launch never reaches the agent. Missing facts, confident answers.
04 · Clash
Two pieces contradict
A March rule meets its June opposite. The model can't ask which one you meant.
Anchor them: too much · wrong or missing · contradictory · false
The scar story · bloat at scale
The agent that drowned in its own data
We gave it everything: multiple data sources, multiple clients, a decision tree before every query
Responses came back slow, and worse, they came back wrong. Textbook bloat.
The fix isn't a smarter model. It's deterministic inputs.
A client dropdown deletes a third of the decision tree
The best context engineering removes decisions from the model entirely
Four techniques keep it clean
Write · Select · Compress · Isolate
01
WriteSave it outside the window so it survives. The crown note goes to memory, so it exists next week
02
SelectPull only what this step needs. The Thursday appointment, not the entire calendar
03
CompressSummarize what grew too long. A month-long thread becomes a paragraph of what matters
04
IsolateGive the messy subtask its own window. What subagents are for: the clutter can't spread
The context window is precious. Defend it.
Retrieval war story two · legal grade fidelity
The 73 Page Policy Document
Answers must quote the policy word for word. Chunking by meaning slices sentences apart.
Brute force worked: pull a big chunk so full sentences survive. Then it got very expensive.
The fix was a diet, not a redesign: shrink the chunk, add a verification loop
Correct first. Then cheaper.

The email drafter · my first retrieval bug
A vector database returns whatever is most similar to what you sent in
I fed the incoming question into the search. Similar to a question: other questions
Not good responses. Things that sounded like the input.
The fix: search on the question, return the human's reply from its metadata
Search on what comes in. Return what should go out.
Retrieval war story one · the proposals database
Past proposals and quotes, drafted from precedent. The source data was not standardized.
Metadata tagging, re-ranking, advanced back-end tricks. The retrieval stayed poor.
No technique on the retrieval side can repair chaos on the data side
The fix was to quit the project. A real option, not a defeat.
If the data's a mess, the mess goes in the scope, or the project doesn't go at all
