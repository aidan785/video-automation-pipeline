The discipline that makes it provable
Capture the Baseline Before You Build
01
The number always existsUntracked? Get the yes first: "if we moved this number, would that provide value?"
02
It comes from the client, self-reportedTheir internal metric. You can't dig it out yourself, and you don't need to.
Then stress test the number
Resolution time: 5 hours to 1 hour. What would that do for your business? Your customers? Your bottom line?
Ask that of 2 or 3 candidate numbers. One separates itself.
One number to move. Target agreed out loud.
"You're at 25 leads booked per week. If we added 20, is that the number you'd like to see increase?"
On the employed track it's worth even more: the agreed number is how your work gets seen.
When the real world gets messy
Three Situations You'll Hit
01
"Time saved" wants to be the headlineSupporting evidence only. 20 hours a week saved is an expense number in disguise.
02
The owner shrugs at every metricThe owner isn't in the weeds. Get the operator in the room. They know the number.
03
They won't commit to one numberAsk why. Then monitor three in production and let the North Star emerge from the data.
Refuses measurement entirely? Client: I'd walk. Boss: negotiate for the number like your review depends on it.
