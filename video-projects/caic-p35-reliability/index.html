The Seven Defenses
01
RetryTry it again. Most failures are accidents.
02
FallbackDrop to a backup: an alternate model, a saved answer, or a human.
03
Guardrails in codeOutputs checked, off-limits actions blocked in code, not in the prompt.
04
Human in the loopA flag that pauses the system and hands off anything high-stakes.
05
Stopping conditionsCaps on steps, tokens, cost. Nothing runs away from you.
06
Graceful degradationA safe state per failure, decided before it happens.
07
LoggingA durable record of every run: what came in, what it decided, what went out.
The non-negotiables on every build: retries, fallbacks, and logging
The one I deliberately don't default on
Hard Cost Caps
More throughput usually means it's working. Why cap the thing succeeding?
A cap that trips mid-month saves nothing: it strands the team for two weeks
01
VisibilityExact cost per run and per month, for you and the client. Dashboards and logs, not caps.
02
Match protection to the triggerPublic can hammer you: per-user cap or cooldown. Your own systems: cap nothing, monitor everything.
03
Make it the client's decisionCost per run, the monthly picture, what a cap would and wouldn't protect.
"What should we expect this to cost?" gets answered confidently, with scenarios

Rolling Out in Slices
Never zero to one hundred. Each slice ships behind a confidence gate.
Onboarding goes live when onboarding has earned it. Booking waits its turn.
Internal tools: ship the draft, gather the data. Customer-facing: higher gate, slower rollout.
Production-ready is a function of confidence and risk
