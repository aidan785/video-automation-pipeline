Define
Sit down with the client’s expert. Write down what good looks like, what the agent must never do, and who has the final say when you disagree.
The expert
Whoever lives in the process
Usually the operator, not the business owner
Most of it is already in your audit and your SOW. Pull it onto one page.
The page leads with the safety promise. The SOW number sits right under it.
Never assume. If you don’t know, ask.

Step three of nine
Read and annotate
Run the agent on every scenario. Then read every row.
The input, what the agent said, and what its tools actually returned. The reply can look perfect while the lookup came back empty.
On every failure, a note
What the agent did. What it should have done. How you know.
Write it for a stranger.
“Invented info” is a reminder to yourself, not a note
These notes are the raw material for every category and every check after this

Step five of nine
Build evaluators
Stop being the only grader. For each failure that matters, build a check that catches it without you.
Objective
Code checks it
Did the lookup return the right order?
Judgment
A second model checks it: the judge
Did the agent claim something the data doesn’t support?
Give the judge the same evidence you used: policies, source data, tool results. Not just the input and the output.
Every check returns pass or fail with a reason. One check per failure. Some come straight from the SOW.
You are training a version of your own judgment

Step six of nine · Calibrate
You didn’t trust the agent, so you built a judge. Now you have two things you don’t trust.
Run it on rows you already labeled. Compare its answers to yours.
A judge that passes everything agrees with you most of the time and missed every failure
What matters: does it catch the failures you caught, and stay quiet on the passes?
Judge wrong? Fix its prompt with rules, not by pasting in the example. You wrong? Reread your labels.
Until the failures the client can’t forgive get caught every time

Step eight of nine · Observe
Production monitoring. The agent goes live with its most important checks running beside it on a sample of traffic.
Write down the failure rate you will accept. Name who gets pinged when it spikes.
The kill switch already exists: the feature flag from Pillar Four
First month: watch it every day
The scariest failures don’t throw an error. They look green.
Most of the time the client notices first. Tell them up front there is a loop for it.
