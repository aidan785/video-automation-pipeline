The diagnose step
Walk the map twice
Walk one · forward
At every step: what could happen here that would be wrong?
No clever technique. Brute force it, step by step. The should-never-happen list is short and gets clear fast.
“My tooth is killing me, do I need to come in?” filed as a reschedule request. One wrong category, a patient in pain sitting in a queue.
Walk two · backward
Start with the stakeholder: what version of this going wrong gets somebody fired?
Take the never-event and trace it back through the flow to where the mistake would be born
Both walks land on the sorting step. That's where the protection goes.
Every finding lands in one of four
The four failure modes
Mode one
Wrong output
Would a competent human have produced something materially different here?
Misfiles the message, invents a fact, drafts a reply nobody would write. The tooth-pain text in the reschedule pile.
Mode two
Exposed data
List everything that can see the data at this step: every tool, model, API, person. Then look at the list.
Retrieval needs one patient's status. If its credential reaches the full chart or payment history, those go on the list.
Mode three
Bad action taken
What can this step alter that a human would have to undo?
An AIS email agent misread its task and sent roughly 150,000 emails. It had a send tool it never should have had. Brightwater's version: the booking step.
Mode four
No audit trail
If the practice called about one case from six weeks ago and asked why, could I answer with evidence?
Something happened and you can't explain it afterward. Go look at the logs.
You won't prevent every failure
When this fails, how bad is the bad?
Same step · safe failure
A reschedule request filed under general questions
A few minutes of front-desk cleanup. Annoying, cheap, recoverable.
Same step · seriously bad failure
The tooth-pain message filed as a reschedule
Same kind of mistake. The cost lands on a patient's health.
Sort failures by what they cost, and design so the expensive ones can't complete on their own
Make failures loud, not invisible. A system that fails and tells you is a system you can run.

Why only four?
Everything folds into these four. Too much context produces a wrong output, exposes data, or triggers a bad action
At every step of any build, you only have four checks to run
Diagnosis stops being an open-ended worry session and becomes a checklist you can finish
Two will dominate your working life
Wrong output and bad action taken
Nearly every build goes through both. Hunt for those hardest.
Audit trail is the most solvable of the four, but only from day one. You can't retrofit March once it's September.
