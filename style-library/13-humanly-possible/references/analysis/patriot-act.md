# Patriot Act with Hasan Minhaj: motion graphics analysis

Source: `video/patriot-act.mp4` (9:00, 1280x720, 23.976 fps). Burst sheets are 8 fps, so 1 burst frame = 3 source frames = 0.125 s. Note: `scene_times.txt` was empty for this video, so all times come from the 2 s uniform sheets and are refined with bursts (expect about +/-1 s on uniform-only times).

## One-paragraph read

Patriot Act is a host standing in a virtual set: a full-frame LED-wall plate behind him (printing press with a giant blackletter "The Chronicle" masthead, duotoned teal with a red/peach wash at top left). The plate itself is the graphic. It swaps to a new themed plate when the topic changes (newspaper collage, navy data wall, peach-and-teal city skyline), and every swap hides inside a camera cut plus a blocky mosaic transition. On top of that sit a small set of very consistent graphic objects: big heavy geometric-sans stat numbers in a peach-to-salmon gradient, a navy source tab with a salmon chevron in the top left corner, navy chart labels with salmon accents, amber name tags on collage photos, hand-drawn amber circles, a 3-line chapter title with white "redaction" bars, and screenshots (tweets, articles, broadcast clips) placed flat beside the host or framed full-screen with a copper gradient border. The host stays in the frame for almost every graphic; the graphics are laid out around his body, never on his face. It feels hand-made because the backgrounds are collaged from real print (halftone dots, paper texture, film-stock edge text like "EXTRA FINE 400 FILM PRO"), because every number assembles letter by letter, and because nothing ever just fades on.

## Motion grammar

- **Easing feel:** snappy in, then a very slow drift. Every element lands in 6 to 12 source frames (0.25 to 0.5 s) with a fast ease-out, then the plate or collage keeps creeping (slow push or pan, a few % over the hold) so the frame is never dead. No bounce, no overshoot you can see, no spins.
- **Typical entrance:** 2 to 4 burst frames (6 to 12 source frames). Bigger events (chapter title, mosaic) take about 8 burst frames (1 s) but are made of fast sub-steps.
- **What moves vs holds:** the host holds (he is live action). The plate moves slowly. Graphic objects arrive fast and then hold perfectly still. Tags and labels never move once placed.
- **Text reveal:** numbers and single words assemble **per character** (each glyph arrives from a slightly offset, blurred, larger state, staggered left to right, 1 burst frame apart). Labels under numbers reveal **per line**. Titles reveal through **bars** (white blocks that grow then retract). Body copy (tweets, articles) is never animated; screenshots arrive whole.
- **Relation to cuts:** plate changes are always hidden behind a camera change (medium to wide or wide to medium), and the mosaic starts one frame after the cut, so the eye reads the cut and the background swap as one event. Graphic objects usually exit on a cut rather than animating out. Cut-out photos and lower thirds can persist across a plate change (Julie K. Brown at 4:22 to 4:32). Stats get re-laid-out when the camera changes so they always sit on the empty side of the host (8.8 MILLION appears beside the close-up, on the open side).
- **Clip handling:** archival clips go full frame inside a copper gradient border with a date chip; consecutive clips are joined by a single overexposed white flash frame (5:28).

## Signature moves

### 1. Mosaic plate swap (the "block shuffle")
At a camera cut, the background swaps to a new themed plate through a grid of horizontal rectangles. Rebuild at 1920x1080: split the plate area into 5 rows of 216 px; each row is cut into 2 to 4 rectangles of random width (200 to 900 px, seeded). Each rectangle shows a crop of the new plate, offset sideways by 40 to 120 px and slightly scaled (1.05), and pops in at full opacity in a seeded order over 6 to 9 frames (0.25 to 0.375 s at 24 fps), 1 to 2 frames apart. Then all rectangles snap to their true offset in one final frame, revealing the clean new plate. Host is a separate layer above (keyed), untouched. Title text (PRIVATE EQUITY at 8:08) can ride in on the same rectangles, so the word arrives as part of the background change.

### 2. Redaction-bar chapter title
3 lines of heavy caps, justified into a block (x 315 to 1612, cap height about 105 px, line gap about 40 px). For each line: a white bar starts as a 6 px sliver at a seeded x (alternating left and right ends), grows horizontally to the full line width in 3 burst frames (9 source frames), then shrinks back from one side revealing the white text behind it over another 3 burst frames. Each line is staggered by 1 burst frame. Bars stop short and leave a solid white rectangle filling the leftover space at the end or start of each line (the "redacted" stubs), so the final title reads as a solid justified block. Whole thing about 1 s, holds about 2 s, exits on a cut.

### 3. Per-character stat assemble + source tab
Number (cap height about 140 px, heavy geometric sans, vertical gradient peach #E9B19A top to salmon #D19D8F bottom, shadow 0 4px 12px rgba(0,0,0,.25)) placed on the side of the frame away from the host's face, baseline about 70% down. Each glyph starts at scale 1.4, y -30 px, opacity 0, blur 8 px and lands in 4 frames; stagger 3 frames per glyph, left to right. A 2-line caps label (about 36 px, same gradient) reveals per line 3 frames after the last glyph lands, by a left-to-right clip wipe. In the same frame as the first glyph, a source tab pops into the top left corner: navy box flush to the top and left frame edges (about 190 x 110 px, or wider for long sources), white bold caps 2 lines, with a salmon right-pointing triangle notched into its left edge. No exit animation; cut away.

### 4. Collage page with name tag and hand-drawn circle
The plate is a collaged newspaper page (masthead at top, headline, columns, cut-out photo panel in the right 40%). The camera slowly pushes in (scale 1.00 to 1.04 over the hold). A name tag (bold serif, sentence case, about 40 px, black on amber #D6993E, 470 x 105 px) arrives at scale 1.6 and shrinks to 1.0 in 3 burst frames, overlapping the photo's lower-left corner, first white then flipping to amber fill. Then a rough brush ring (about 420 px, amber, 14 px stroke with dry-brush breaks) writes on clockwise around the face in about 12 source frames. Page-to-page changes fly the camera through the stacked paper layers with motion blur in 6 burst frames.

### 5. Tweet / screenshot pop beside the host
A flat screenshot at about 42% frame width placed right of the host, vertically centred around 50%. Enters from about 25% scale, slightly low, to 100% in 6 source frames, then settles about 2% up and left over the next 6 frames. No shadow, no border, no rotation. Successive tweets replace each other in the same slot by a hard swap.

### 6. Cut-out guest + bleed-off lower third
A real photo cutout of the person being discussed stands in the set beside the host (full height, right 35% of frame). The lower third is a navy box anchored to the bottom edge (bleeding off frame), with an 8 px salmon bar at its left, 2 lines of bold caps in salmon (name, role). It arrives with the cutout and persists across a background change.

## Fit for Humanly Possible

1. **Mosaic plate swap:** fits as a chapter transition if kept short. Use 5 rows of flat rectangles in brick #7A1E1E and oxblood #4B1526 tones (crops of the next plate, or flat colour blocks), pop in over 6 to 9 frames with cubic-bezier(.2,.9,.25,1), no blur. Use it only at chapter boundaries hidden behind a camera cut, never mid-sentence. Our flat surfaces make this read even cleaner than theirs.
2. **Redaction-bar title:** strong fit for chapter cards ("THE ONE THING SHE USES AI FOR"). Bricolage Grotesque 800 caps in cream #FFF0E6, bars in cream too; on oxblood ground. Make the one key word gold #FFC145 and keep one orange #FF8A4C bar stub as the frame's single accent. Keep timings (about 1 s in) but drop any blur. Radius 0 on bars (they must read as type blocks, not UI).
3. **Per-character stat + source tab:** fits. Number in Bricolage 800, gold #FFC145 (the one gold number), label in Inter Tight 600 cream. Replace the gradient with flat colour (our surfaces are flat). Glyph entrance as scale 1.15 to 1 plus y 20 px to 0 over 4 frames, 2-frame stagger, no blur (blur and big scale read as their style, not ours). Source tab becomes a brick #7A1E1E chip with 6 px radius on the free corners and an orange #FF8A4C 6 px left bar, Inter Tight 600 caps 18 px. Good for "saved 6 hours a week" moments.
4. **Collage page + name tag + circle:** partial fit. The halftone newspaper collage is their subject matter, not ours. Keep the method: a slow-push full-frame plate made of the guest's real artifacts (their spreadsheet, their phone screenshot, their shop photo) on brick, a name tag in cream with 6 px radius, Bricolage 800, and a hand-drawn ring in orange #FF8A4C written on in 12 frames around the one detail that matters. The brush ring fits our hand-made goal; keep it to one per frame.
5. **Screenshot pop beside the host:** fits directly and is probably our most-used card (the AI chat, the prompt, the result). 42% width, 10 px radius, flat cream or white card, no shadow and no glass, pop from 0.9 to 1.0 scale with our bezier in 6 frames, small 2% settle. Do not start from 25% scale; that much travel reads like a bounce.
6. **Cut-out guest + lower third:** the cut-out-person-in-set idea does not fit an interview show (our guest is on camera already). The lower third does: brick box anchored to the bottom edge, 6 px top-corner radius, 8 px orange bar at left, name in Bricolage 800 cream, role in Inter Tight cream 80%. Enter by a 6-frame upward pop, persist across cuts within the same speaker.

Does not fit: the duotone teal/red LED-wall plates, the copper gradient clip borders, the white flash frame between clips, and the peach gradient type. Those are the show's identity, and gradients and flashes break our flat-surface rule.
