# Ali Abdaal: "How I use AI to save 10+ hours per week" (first 9:00)

Source: `video/ali-abdaal.mp4`, 1280x720. Bursts in `bursts/ali-abdaal/`, hero frames in `picks/`.

## One-paragraph read

The graphics language is "friendly productivity app": a warm cream ground (#F7F2EA-ish) with soft lilac and lavender blobs, rounded cards (roughly 16 to 24px at 1080p) with soft drop shadows, a soft serif for kinetic captions and quotes, and a semibold rounded sans for chapter titles. Everything is built around the idea that AI is a tool you watch him use, so the strongest moves are about presenting real software: a phone mockup docked to the right of the talking head that mirrors what he is doing on his real phone, a magnifier bubble that lifts one line of AI output off that phone, full-screen desktop screen recordings with a small rounded face-cam PIP, an isolate-and-blur treatment that pulls one UI menu out of a blurred dashboard, and a node diagram of app tiles joined by dashed arrows to explain a workflow. Motion is quick and soft (short ease-outs, small blur on entry, almost no overshoot) and nearly every graphic is cut on rather than animated off. The one move that really sells "hand-made" is the pill lower third that grows into the container for the next B-roll shot: graphics morph into each other instead of popping in and out.

## Motion grammar

- Easing feel: snappy ease-out, little to no overshoot. Typical entrance is 3 to 5 frames at 30fps for pills and tiles, about 0.5s for the bigger card morphs. Nothing bounces; the chapter title words are the only thing with blur on entry.
- What moves vs what holds: the frame layout holds and the content inside swaps. The list keeps its cream layout while the video card inside changes footage per item; the phone mockup holds while the app screens inside it change; the PIP holds while the screen recording changes.
- Text reveal: almost always per word, keyed to speech. Two flavors: (1) captions over footage pop each word in fully, (2) cream-card text shows the word ghosted first (about 25 to 30% opacity) then darkens, so the sentence "fills in" like ink.
- Relation to cuts: graphics are usually removed by a cut, not animated off. The moves that do exit get motion (whip blur, panel slide down) so the cut hides inside the motion. The phone mockup and PIP persist across cuts of the underlying footage, which makes long demo stretches feel continuous.
- Transitions between graphic states are morphs (pill into card, white panel up and down) rather than separate in/out animations.

## Signature moves

1. **Pill grows into the next shot's frame.** Lower-third pill at bottom center (x 780 to 1140, y 900 to 1000 at 1080p, radius 24) reveals its label by width (scaleX from 0.1 with the label clipped, 0.15s), corner blobs scale in 0.1s later. On the next beat, the same element animates to x 40 to 1880, y 40 to 1040 over 0.5s (ease-out), fill stays cream while a video layer inside fades from 0 to 1 at 40% of the tween. The border/margin and corner blobs remain as the frame of the B-roll. One DOM node, two states.
2. **Phone mirror docked to the talking head.** Device frame 420x900 at x 1440 to 1860, y 60 to 1020, shadow 0 20px 40px rgba(0,0,0,.3). A screen recording of the real phone plays inside, timed to his hands. It sits there for minutes across cuts so the demo reads as one continuous action.
3. **Magnifier bubble on a single AI line.** Over the phone, a white ellipse about 420x240 overlapping the left bezel by about 60px; inside, the one line he is reading set at 1.4x the phone's type size. Phone screen behind dims to about 70%. Each new line is a hard swap, not an animation.
4. **Isolate one UI element from a blurred screen.** Full screenshot blurred (about 12px) and lightened (white at 40%); the single relevant panel is a sharp copy at 1.1x scale with a soft shadow, cursor animates inside it. Next element replaces with a 0.15s fade.
5. **Ink-fill quote card.** Cream full-screen, block top-left at x 60, y 60, max width 1500, serif 72px, leading 1.05. Every word is in the layout from the start at opacity 0 and turns on to 0.25 at its spoken word start, then to 1.0 over 0.3s. The key clause switches to the accent color.
6. **Node diagram with drawn dashed arrows.** App tiles 250x180, a 44px colored header strip with the app name and a white body with the app icon, soft colored glow shadow. Dashed SVG path between tiles draws on via stroke-dashoffset over 0.4s, arrowhead pops at the end, the next tile pops (scale 0.9 to 1, 0.15s) at the arrow tip. Whole group pans left as it grows to keep it centered.

## Fit for Humanly Possible

1. **Pill into frame: fits well.** Brick #7A1E1E pill, cream Bricolage 800 label, 14px radius; grow it into a brick-margined frame (10px radius on the video) around the B-roll of the guest's real tool. Drop the lilac blobs; if we want a corner accent, use one small orange #FF8A4C tab. Width reveal 0.15s plus the 0.5s morph on cubic-bezier(.2,.9,.25,1), no overshoot.
2. **Phone mirror: fits, and is the most useful move for our show.** Guests will show their phone. Dock a plain device frame (or simply a 14px-radius screen card, no glossy bezel) on the side away from the guest's face; it persists through the guest's answer. Keep it flat with a simple hard-ish shadow, no glass. Oxblood ground only if we pull the guest into a split; over footage, the card alone.
3. **Magnifier bubble: fits with changes.** Replace the white ellipse with a cream #FFF0E6 rounded rect (14px), Inter Tight at about 40px, the one quoted AI line, with the single key word in gold #FFC145 on a dark sliver or underlined in orange. Pop-and-settle in 0.2s, swap lines on a cut. This is our "here is the exact prompt / answer" device.
4. **Isolate-and-blur UI: fits.** Blur the real screen recording, tint with oxblood at 60% instead of white, lift the one panel sharp at 1.08x with a 10px radius. Our "look at this one button" move. No glow around the lifted panel.
5. **Ink-fill quote: fits strongly.** Oxblood ground, cream Bricolage 800 text at about 88px, ghosted at 25% cream then full on the spoken word; one phrase in gold #FFC145 rather than a whole salmon clause (gold is for one word or number, so keep the highlight short). Add film grain. Use it for the guest's best line.
6. **Node diagram: fits as a "how they wired it" card.** Brick tiles with cream headers, Bricolage labels, dashed cream arrows, one orange arrow for the step that matters. Drop the glow shadows (no glows); use flat tiles on oxblood with grain.

Does not fit: the per-word blur fly-in on the purple chapter title (too floaty and a drift rather than a pop) and the teal kinetic captions with drop shadow over the footage (cheap-looking against our flat palette; if we caption, use a cream word on a brick tab instead).
