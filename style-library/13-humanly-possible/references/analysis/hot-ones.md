# Hot Ones (First We Feast): motion graphics analysis

Source: `video/hot-ones.mp4` (7:00, 1280x720, 23.976 fps). This is the 2017-era behind-the-scenes / Brett Baker interview episode, not a celebrity episode, but it contains the full graphics package (lower thirds, Scoville cards, neon open, yellow archive frames, tweet cards). Burst sheets are 8 fps, so 1 burst frame = 3 source frames = 0.125 s. `scene_times.txt` was empty, so times come from the 2 s uniform sheets refined with bursts (expect about +/-1 s on uniform-only times).

Palette correction to the brief: in this episode the graphics are not red/gold. They are **highlighter yellow #F9F10A**, **flat white paint splatter**, **neon orange-red #F45F1B outlines**, **warm neon yellow type**, and **black**. Red and gold come from the sauce bottles and wings, not the graphics.

## One-paragraph read

Hot Ones graphics are loud, flat and physical, and they barely animate. The studio is pure black, so every graphic either sits on black as a glowing neon sign (lower thirds, Scoville card, the open) or cuts to a full-frame highlighter-yellow field with white paint splats (archive clips, tweets, photos, the logo sting). There are almost no eases: things hard-cut on with the shot and hold. The motion budget is spent on three tactile moments only: the paint splat that grows into the First We Feast logo, the neon tubes flickering on, and the neon flames in the logo and name boxes igniting. Type is condensed (Bebas / Oswald feel) for documentary supers and a rounded neon-sign sans for the show package. The hand-made feel comes from real-world references: neon tubes, splatter paint, a yellow legal pad, a mascot bug that occasionally animates in the corner.

## Motion grammar

- **Easing feel:** almost no easing. The package is cut-driven: supers, lower thirds, Scoville cards and caption text appear on the first frame of a shot and leave on its last. When things do move (archive inset, tweet, photo slides), it is a 3 to 6 source-frame linear-feeling slide with a hard stop.
- **Typical entrance:** 0 frames (hard on) for type; 3 to 6 frames for framed media; 1 to 2 s for the two "ceremony" moments (splat logo, neon open).
- **What moves vs holds:** boxes, type and frames hold rigidly. Light moves instead: neon tubes flicker on, flame icons ignite, the Scoville bottle is top-lit from dark. That is the core idea: **animate the light, not the layout**.
- **Text reveal:** none per letter. Type is always whole. Only the neon open "reveals" type, and it does so with tube flicker, not wipes.
- **Relation to cuts:** graphics are locked to edit points. The yellow field arrives on a hard cut; the media inside it slides in. Nothing persists across cuts except the corner bug. Yellow fields are used as a punctuation colour: every aside (old clip, fan tweet, childhood photo) goes to yellow, then hard cuts back to the black studio, which makes the yellow feel like a page turn.

## Signature moves

### 1. Neon-box lower third with igniting icons
Bottom centre box, 850 x 180 px at 1920x1080 (x 535 to 1385, y 810 to 990), radius about 14 px, 6 px tube stroke in #F45F1B with a soft outer glow, fill rgba(20,8,4,.55) so the set reads through. Name in rounded sans caps 56 px, warm white with yellow tint, centred; handle 30 px beneath. Two flame icons (about 70 px tall) inside the left and right ends. Box and type hard-on on the shot cut. At +24 frames, each icon flicks from a 30% opacity unlit outline to fully lit over 2 to 3 frames (one flicker: lit, dim, lit).

### 2. Highlighter-yellow aside field
On a hard cut, the frame becomes flat #F9F10A with 3 or 4 white splatter shapes bleeding in from the edges (biggest bottom right behind the bug, others left and top). The content (old clip at 74% width, tweet at 66% width, or a print photo at 34% width) slides in from the left over 3 to 6 frames and stops dead. Hold, then hard cut back to the studio. Splat shapes change per insert so it never looks like a template.

### 3. Paint-splat logo sting
Hard cut to yellow. Frame 0: a small white paint blob in centre. Over about 24 frames the blob splats outward with droplets (stop-motion feel, about 8 fps), a black ink ring draws around it with a drip on the top right, then brush letters paint in stroke by stroke over another 24 frames, then the mascot pops in the O. Hold about 1 s, hard cut. Rebuild as a pre-drawn SVG: blob as 6 to 8 stepped shape keyframes (step easing, no tween), ring as a stroke-dashoffset draw, letters as stroke draws with 2-frame stagger.

### 4. Scoville card: card holds, the hero gets lit
A tall neon card pinned left (350 x 860 px, x 150, y 110): pepper icon outline with horizontal meter rungs, a green tick showing the level, "SCOVILLE / LEVEL" in 2 lines and the number in green beneath. It is fully on from frame 1. The motion is the product beside it: the bottle starts as a black silhouette and a top light fades it up over about 24 frames. Ritual repeats every round with the same layout, only the number and the meter tick change.

### 5. Neon logo flicker open
Black warm vignette. "FIRST WE FEAST PRESENTS" hard-on, then the HOT ONES tube letters rise from below frame unlit (dark outlines), flicker on (on, off, on over 9 frames), the stack keeps drifting upward about 6% for 2 s, the chicken lights last, the flame plume loops on and off (about 1.1 s cycle).

## Fit for Humanly Possible

1. **Neon-box lower third with igniting icons:** the layout and the "animate the light" idea fit; the neon and glow do not (our rule: no glows or glass on UI). Translate as a flat brick #7A1E1E box, 10 px radius, a solid 4 px orange #FF8A4C outline instead of a tube, name in Bricolage Grotesque 800 cream #FFF0E6, role in Inter Tight cream. The "ignite" becomes one icon (a small AI-spark or tool glyph) that pops from 0.8 to 1.0 scale and from cream outline to orange fill 12 frames after the box lands. Box enters with our 6-frame pop-and-settle rather than hard-on, since our edits are calmer.
2. **Highlighter-yellow aside field:** strong fit as a concept, with our colours. Every aside (the guest's screenshot, an old photo, a quote from a customer) cuts to a full-frame brick #7A1E1E field with film grain, a few flat cream paper scraps or torn-edge shapes bleeding in from the edges, and the media at 66 to 74% width with 10 px radius, sliding in from the left over 6 frames with cubic-bezier(.2,.9,.25,1). Hard cut back to the interview. Never yellow; gold #FFC145 is reserved for one word or number.
3. **Paint-splat logo sting:** fits as our show sting if redrawn: cream paint blob on oxblood, stepped (stop-motion) keyframes, an orange #FF8A4C ink ring, Bricolage 800 wordmark. Keep under 2.5 s. Step easing is fine here because it reads hand-made, not bouncy.
4. **Scoville card ritual:** the repeating-ritual idea fits perfectly for a recurring "AI use" card that appears at the same point each episode (for example "THE ONE AI MOVE" card pinned left with a meter for time saved). Make it a flat brick card, 14 px radius, label in Inter Tight caps cream, the value in gold #FFC145 Bricolage 800. Drop neon; keep "card holds, the hero moves" (the guest's screen recording or product beside it can be the moving part).
5. **Neon flicker open:** does not fit. Neon glow and flicker loops break the flat, no-glow rule. Keep only the structure: a "Humanly Possible presents" line hard-on, then the wordmark rising in with our pop-and-settle.

Does not fit at all: the condensed documentary supers with no box (too generic, and fight our grotesque headline type), the colour-bars gag, the flame composite, and the SMPTE / weather parody pieces. Hot Ones' general hard-on-with-the-cut timing is worth copying for captions and supers only.
