# gbs-memory: "This Woman Remembers Every Day of Her Life" (Great Big Story, 2:42)

Source: `video/gbs-memory.mp4` (720p). All pixel numbers below are measured on the 1280x720 frame and converted to 1920x1080 (x1.5) where a rebuild spec is given.

## One-paragraph read

This one is fully animated (no live action, no interview footage), so the graphics language lives in three places: the type that defines the story's key idea, the series title lockup, and the brand bookends. Everything else is illustration doing the storytelling. The type is sparse and very confident: one condensed all-caps line in butter yellow at the top of a deep indigo frame, then that line literally edits itself down into an acronym, word by word. The title card is a thin white rectangle with the rocket medallion breaking its top edge, a wide tracked geometric bold title inside, and "A GREAT BIG STORY" hanging under it in mixed weights. It opens out of a single line and closes back into a line. Motion is almost entirely hard-on, a 2 to 3 frame snap, then a long dead-still hold; nothing floats or fades. The brand bug (stacked GREAT / BIG / STORY, top right, with a red dot) is absent while the title owns the frame and returns once the story starts. Transitions are illustrated match moves (zoom into the pyramid, the room iris-closing into a triangle) plus full-frame diagonal hatch textures, never a dissolve.

## Motion grammar

- Easing feel: snappy on, dead still. Every entrance is 1 to 3 frames (at 24 fps that is about 40 to 125 ms) or a mask reveal of about 9 frames. There is no drift, no slow push on type, and no fades anywhere in the graphics.
- Typical entrance: title card about 9 frames (line, then horizontal center-out mask). Medallion pop about 2 to 3 frames. Definition line 1 frame (hard cut on).
- What moves vs what holds: the illustration underneath keeps animating (the pyramid fills with color, the scene behind breathes) while the type holds perfectly still. The type is the anchor; the world moves.
- Text reveals: never per letter. The one clever exception is the acronym edit, which is per word, subtractive (words leave, the initial stays and briefly enlarges).
- Graphics vs cuts: the title sequence plays over one continuous illustrated shot. The texture plates themselves act as the cuts. The brand bug drops out while a title card owns the frame and returns at the next scene.
- Exits mirror entrances: opened from a line, closes to a line. That symmetry is the signature.

## Signature moves

1. **Line-born title box.** A 2 px white rectangle, 80% frame width by 35% height, centered (at 1920x1080: x 189 to 1730, y 348 to 730). Entrance: a 2 px vertical line drops 0 to about 60 px from the medallion (4 frames), then the box and title reveal from the center outward with a `clip-path: inset(0 50% 0 50%)` to `inset(0 0 0 0)` over 9 frames. Medallion (75 px circle) sits centered on the top stroke with a gap cut out of the stroke around it. Sub-label 22 px wide-tracked under the box. Hold 3 s. Exit: animate the box `scaleY` 1 to 0.01 about its center over 6 frames with the title inside a wrapper that squashes with it, hold the resulting line 6 frames, then `scaleX` the line to 0 in 4 frames.
2. **Subtractive acronym edit.** Full term set in one line at top center (cap height about 82 px at 1080). After a 2 s hold, step through the words: each step removes the tail of one word, leaves its initial plus a period, and that initial swaps for 1 frame to a wider, heavier cut at about 120% size before settling. The remaining words slide left (x tween 4 frames) to close the gap, and the whole line re-centers at the end. 4 steps in about 0.6 s total.
3. **Brand on the object.** The brand mark lands on the story's hero object (the pyramid) instead of in a corner: wordmark on first, medallion pops 0 to 1 on the next 2 to 3 frames, then the title box grows out of that medallion. The brand is the seed the title grows from.
4. **Mask-iris outro.** The final living scene is held inside a shape mask that shrinks to a tiny hatched glyph in about 1 s, then the brand line art draws on stroke by stroke (`stroke-dashoffset` over 2 s) with the wordmark clipped up from below.
5. **Bug yields to the title.** Corner bug (stacked wordmark, about 135 px wide at 1080, 100 px from the right edge, 55 px from the top) is hidden while any full title or definition card is on screen and returns hard-on at the first story shot.

## Fit for Humanly Possible

1. **Line-born title box: fits, with changes.** Use cream #FFF0E6 for the 2 px stroke (go 3 px at 1080) on the oxblood #4B1526 ground or over footage, title in Bricolage Grotesque 800 sentence case or caps, cream. Replace the red medallion with our mark in a small brick #7A1E1E or orange #FF8A4C disc (the orange is then the frame's one accent). Give the box 6 px corner radius to match our radii. Swap their linear mask for our cubic-bezier(.2,.9,.25,1) on the center-out reveal (about 10 frames) and on the collapse to a line. This is the best candidate for our episode title card.
2. **Subtractive acronym edit: fits well for "the one AI tool" beat.** Set the full phrase in Inter Tight or Bricolage, cream, then edit it down to the short name the guest uses (for example a long tool description collapsing to a 3 to 4 letter name). The kept initials can flash in gold #FFC145 for their pop frame, which also satisfies "gold for one word". Keep the pop at about 110% to 115% scale for 2 frames with our pop-and-settle curve, no bounce.
3. **Brand on the object: fits in spirit.** We are mostly live action, so "the object" becomes a held hero frame of the guest's hands, tool or workplace. Drop our mark on that object first, then grow the title box from it. Do not put the mark over a face (marathons shows the risk of that).
4. **Mask-iris outro: partial fit.** A shape iris is fine if the shape is a flat 10 or 14 px rounded rectangle (our card language), not a triangle or a decorative glyph, and it must close in under 1 s with no long fade. The stroke-draw logo is fine if our mark has a line version; otherwise skip and use a hard pop-on.
5. **Bug yields to the title: fits as a rule.** Adopt directly: hide our corner bug whenever a tier-1 card or title owns the frame.

Does not fit: the condensed yellow-on-indigo illustration palette and the full-frame hatch textures (we are flat oxblood with film grain, not illustrated grounds), the 3D extruded illustrated letters, and the YouTube end-screen panel layout (keep it only if we publish with end screens, rebuilt in oxblood and brick).
