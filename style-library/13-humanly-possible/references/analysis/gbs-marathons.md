# gbs-marathons: "I Ran 365 Marathons In 365 Days" (Great Big Story, 4:48)

Source: `video/gbs-marathons.mp4` (720p). Pixel numbers are measured on the 1280x720 frame; rebuild specs convert to 1920x1080 (x1.5).

## One-paragraph read

This is the live-action version of the Great Big Story system and it is very close to our format: an everyday person, a cold open of B-roll, then a branded title card, then sit-down interviews intercut with family and friends. The graphics are few, small and quiet, and almost all of them are type set in one wide-tracked geometric bold (Futura / Brandon feel) in white with a single short red dash as the only color accent. The title card is the same line-born outline box as the animated films, laid over a portrait of the subject. Place and name supers are tiny, all caps, two lines, with a 40 px red dash under them. Facts and numbers get exactly two treatments: a giant white counter that rolls up over a running shot, and "visual math" plates on a charcoal paper ground (world map zoom, one mountain multiplied into a grid). Archive photos sit untouched and centered on the same charcoal ground. Captions are burned in on every spoken line, white sentence case, centered, no box. The brand is everywhere but never loud: a stacked corner bug for the whole runtime, the medallion only on the title and the final wordmark, and a red dot as the recurring brand atom. Motion is hard cuts and 2 to 3 frame snaps; the only fade in the film is the in-memoriam card.

## Motion grammar

- Easing feel: snappy and economical. Supers appear in 1 to 3 frames with a per-line stagger of about 1 frame each (roughly 40 ms at 24 fps); title open and close take about 9 frames each. Nothing drifts except the brand wordmark's small rise before the title opens and the slow map zoom.
- What moves vs what holds: type holds still over locked-off or gently moving shots. When a number moves, the number itself is the motion (the counter rolls), and the framing stays locked.
- Text reveals: per line, not per letter or word. Name first, role next, red dash last, top to bottom.
- Numbers: either a rolling counter over the subject in action, or an abstract plate on charcoal that turns the number into a picture (distance becomes a map, elevation becomes a stack of mountains). The plates count by adding one object per frame.
- Graphics vs cuts: supers often survive one cut (place super across two aerials, Bill's name across a punch-in), which makes them feel placed by an editor rather than glued to a clip. The title card sits on a single held portrait. Exits are mostly hard cuts on the edit, not animated outs.
- Brand rhythm: no brand during the 34 s cold open; medallion, then title, then the bug lives in the corner for the rest of the film; at the end the bug dissolves to its red dot, which becomes the final medallion. Open and close are mirrored.

## Signature moves

1. **Wordmark-to-title morph.** At 1920x1080: medallion 150 px disc centered at y 435, wordmark 60 px cap at y 620, both hard on. Wordmark rises 9 px over 12 frames. Frame 15: a 3 px white line grows from center to 1505 px wide (x 207 to 1712) at y 393 in 4 frames. Frames 19 to 27: the box bottom edge drops from y 393 to y 687 (`scaleY` from top origin), title (75 px cap, wide tracked) unmasks with it, medallion shrinks to 90 px and parks on the top edge (stroke has a gap behind it), wordmark scales to 33 px and slides to y 740 under the box. Hold 3 s. Exit: box `scaleY` to a line about its center in 9 frames, hold 9 frames, cut.
2. **Three-step super with a red dash.** Name: bold caps 39 px cap height, tracking +6%, at x 192, top y 108. Role: 25 px cap, 6 px below. Dash: 60x8 px red, 18 px below the role. Stagger name, role, dash by 1 frame each, no movement, no fade. Same unit centered at the bottom for place supers (city large, country small, dash under). Hold 3 to 4 s and let it ride across a cut.
3. **Rolling counter over action.** A 195 px cap heavy numeral, pure white, centered on the subject's torso in a locked running shot. Count 0 to target over about 2.3 s with a strong ease-out (`power3.out` feel, last 5% of the value takes the last 30% of the time), hold the final value 1.4 s, hard cut. No label on screen; the voiceover names the unit.
4. **Visual-math plate on charcoal.** Flat #2B2B2B ground with fine paper grain. Show one hero object (a cutout photo) at about 60% width for 1.2 s, then shrink it with 2 frames of motion blur into slot 1 of a 3-column grid and add copies one per frame (42 ms each) in reading order until the count is reached. Variant: a flat pale map that slowly zooms to a labelled destination.
5. **Archive photo on paper.** Personal photos centered, untouched, no frame, no tilt, no shadow, on the same charcoal ground at about 92% frame height, captions still burned over them. Cut on and off.
6. **Brand dot bookend.** Corner bug for the whole body; at the last shot its letters drop away, leaving the red dot, and a full-height stacked wordmark appears with the dot as the medallion; the logo line draws inside the dot over about 12 frames.

## Fit for Humanly Possible

1. **Wordmark-to-title morph: fits, strongest pick for the episode open.** Our mark lands on a held portrait of the guest after the cold open, then grows the outline title box. Stroke in cream #FFF0E6, title in Bricolage Grotesque 800 cream, sub-label in Inter Tight. Medallion becomes a small orange #FF8A4C disc (the frame's one accent). Round the box corners to 10 px. Use our cubic-bezier(.2,.9,.25,1) for the line grow, the box open and the collapse; no overshoot. Keep the medallion off the face (here it sits squarely on Gary's face for 0.6 s, which reads fine for a brand but wrong for a show about the person).
2. **Three-step super with a dash: fits directly.** Name in Bricolage 800, role in Inter Tight semibold, the dash in orange as the one accent. If the role mentions the AI tool, set the tool name in gold #FFC145 and switch the dash to cream so there is still only one orange element. Optional: put the name unit on a brick #7A1E1E flat plate with 6 px radius when the background is busy. Keep the 1-frame stagger but give each line a 6 to 8 px rise with our pop-and-settle so it is not a dead hard-on.
3. **Rolling counter: fits, ideal for the "how much time / money it saved" number.** Numeral in Bricolage 800 cream, and the final settled value can flip to gold #FFC145 on the landing frame. Count with our ease (pop-and-settle on the final frame, a 104% to 100% scale settle over 6 frames, no bounce). Over footage only if the shot is locked off; otherwise put it on an oxblood plate.
4. **Visual-math plate: fits, with our ground.** Replace charcoal with oxblood #4B1526 plus film grain; hero object as a flat cutout or a simple flat icon on a brick card with 14 px radius; the multiply step adds one copy every 2 frames with a short pop. Only one object highlighted in orange. The motion-blur shrink is fine if kept to 2 to 3 frames.
5. **Archive photo on paper: fits.** Guest's personal photos centered on oxblood with grain, 6 px radius on the photo corners, no tilt, no drop shadow, no glow. This is exactly the human, unfussy tone we want.
6. **Brand dot bookend: partial fit.** We can mirror open and close with our mark, but a permanent corner bug for the whole runtime is a channel choice; for Humanly Possible keep the bug small and hide it whenever a tier-1 card is up.

Does not fit: the YouTube end-screen and merch layout, the in-memoriam slow fade (keep it as a rare exception only when the story needs it), and plain white captions with a drop shadow if our caption spec already uses a different treatment (their size and placement, two lines at about 33 px centered low, are still a good reference).
